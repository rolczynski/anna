---

The move is decided, the wording executes it. A short message is dozens of small decisions, every
word, ordering, omission, and none of them show in the final text. The length says nothing about
the work inside. A message has more readers than the recipient: whoever it gets forwarded to, the
record it becomes part of. It is written for the most consequential one. One story across all
channels, and every stated claim survives a fact-check. What the reader infers beyond the claims
is their business. In negotiation nothing is revealed that spends the asymmetry: no plans, no
finances, no alternatives, no deadlines that aren't chosen. The question's shape sets the answers
it can receive: a small concrete ask gets answered, an open-ended one gets silence. Every word
cascades, what it anchors, what it concedes. Tone matches the thread's register, not the stakes.
Formality where the thread was plain signals that something changed. The sent message goes into
the thread word for word, as it went out.
