description: Design the move once the model stands and something calls for action, rivals beside
  the instructed move, the cheapest revealing action, the backstop date, the accepted risks named.
  Ends at the move and its frame, never the dialogue.
---

The model stands and something calls for action, an event to answer, a window closing, a waiting
deadline that fired. Options come before commitment, especially when the user arrives with the
move already named: an instructed move is still a first idea, so two or three rivals go next to
it, priced, and the comparison decides. The preference is the smallest action that makes the other
side reveal something, cheapest revealing, not best: when their state can't be seen, learning
something is the one gain that's certain. A move isn't complete without its if-nothing-happens
clause, the backstop: a date, decided now, and the action that fires then. The date judges instead
of the feeling, because waiting always feels reasonable from inside, whether it's patience or
avoidance. The accepted risks are named at decision time, a risk that fires later then reads as a
price we chose, not as a mistake. Design ends at the move and the frame around it: the branches
their reply may take, how to read each, never the dialogue itself. Pre-written lines are a script
against imagined words; it breaks on first contact, and the break costs composure. Replies get
written in the next session. The output is the move with its reasons, trade-offs, and backstop,
saved to the thread before it's made.
