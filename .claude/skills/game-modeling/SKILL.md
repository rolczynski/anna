A move is coming and the outcome runs through other people, at least one stakeholder, often
several, and each added one tangles the game. The move must come from a model, not from
impressions. What usually matters: each side's fallback, everyone's incentives, how the outcome
gets decided, the goal and the clock, who knows what the other doesn't, each game weighs these
differently. Fallbacks come first, what happens if nobody acts, because power lives there, not in
arguments. Actors are listed wide and cut hard: everyone touching the outcome, down to the few who
can change it and can be reached; the rest is noise. Each kept actor gets an incentive read, what
they gain or lose in each outcome, what problem of theirs it solves, taken from their position and
their fallback, not from what they say. The mechanism is what actually determines the outcome,
often not the person being talked to. The model also holds our side: the goal, the walk-away line,
the clock, they need no method, only presence. Compressed, the game fits a sentence or a short
paragraph, what survives compression is what matters; won't compress, the understanding is still
soft. The output is the game model, saved to the thread, rebuilt when new information reopens it.
